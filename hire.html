Hire me to tune AI for you

A letter, not a CV. The CV is one click away if you need it; this is the thing I would rather send.

I don't like sending out CVs. The ones I have sent never felt like they represented me, and I suspect they never felt like much to the people who received them either. A CV says what I am. A letter says what I saw, and what I would do about it. So here is the letter I would write you, with the parts that depend on you left open.

I see you

You are building something, or you have built it and now it has to be run, sold and kept honest. Somewhere in it there is a language model, or there is about to be, and the gap between “it works in the demo” and “we can stand behind it in front of a customer” is wider than the roadmap admits.

In the real letter this paragraph names the specific thing I noticed after an hour of looking at what you have put in public: a page, a changelog, a job ad, a talk. If an hour of looking yields nothing specific to say, I don't write.

Why I am writing

I am looking for paid work. I have done AI work since before it was cool: NLP and applied machine learning since 2009, language-model systems for the last few years. I think I would be useful to you in particular. You can't know that from a web page, so I would like a conversation.

How I would approach your thing

Not the deck first. The thing itself: run it, load it, find the first part that gives way, and write down what breaks and in what order. Then two or three concrete first moves, in your vocabulary, in the order I would actually do them, working for you and with you. Your letter gets the first ten minutes of the consultation for free.

Where you are exposed, where there is room

One vulnerability and one potential, each stated as a hypothesis we check together rather than a verdict. What I know about your niche, where I think your business is open to the weather, and where I see room that you may not have priced in. This is the paragraph that earns the call. It is also the one most likely to be wrong, which is why it is written so that being wrong costs you nothing.

The brief

I have worked in machine learning since it was called NLP and data science: research lead at Spring Research (2020–2022), owning the full cycle from market data to trained models; earlier data-science and NLP roles before that. In 2024 I was retained by LevEhad as a technical infrastructure consultant through a cloud migration. In 2026 I did an LLM research contract for Move38: ontologies, prompt sets, test benches and harnesses. Alongside that I keep an open practice, including two Elixir/Phoenix services of my own: an LLM inference harness with multi-provider routing, role-specific fallback and bounded concurrency, and a credential gateway in front of the Gemini API. Those are private; I will walk you through the code. The public work is in the tools and the gallery, and at . I work specification-first and leave repositories that someone else can run.

The ask

Thirty minutes in the next two weeks. Better, a coffee, if you are in Jerusalem or passing through. The menu is short:

  • hire me
  • buy a scoped piece of work
  • put me on a retainer

Write to or . Tell me what you are building, and I will spend the hour on it first.

— Pavel Vasilyev
