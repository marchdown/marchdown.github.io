## The letter

**Subject:** Mingla on hotel wifi, and the part-time role

Hi Chris,

I've been reading Mingla: a browser-based networking game for people who find a room full of
strangers hard, running on Phoenix LiveView and Ash, with challenges that steer guests toward
booths and matchmaking that is supposed to put the right two people together at the right
moment. Your February 2025 post wanted a senior Elixir engineer to push the LiveView app
through a scaling year; the September one asks for either a CTO in Stockholm or a part-time
engineer anywhere. I read the second as: the product is past the point where one person's
judgment can be in every room at once, and you'd rather buy continuity of technical judgment
than another pair of hands you have to manage.

I'm writing because I'm looking for paid work and I think I'd be useful to you specifically.
I've worked in machine learning since it was called NLP, and I write Elixir. You can't know
whether that's true from an email, so I'd like a conversation.

If I were starting with you, the first thing I'd do is not read the deck but run the app: get
one event's worth of guests hitting it from a load generator, on a throttled connection, and
find the first thing that falls over at five times your largest event so far. A LiveView app
at a live event has one failure mode that matters, which is two thousand phones on venue wifi
reconnecting their sockets at the same moment during the keynote, and the fix is usually
measured degradation (what the guest sees when the socket is gone for eight seconds), not a
rewrite. I'd write that up as a two-page "what breaks and in what order" memo you could hand
to whoever you eventually hire as CTO. Second, I'd write the one-page data-handling note your
sales team can send when an ABB-sized procurement office asks what "intent data shared with
organizers and sponsors" means under GDPR; the site doesn't have one yet, and in the EU that
question arrives before the contract does.

Where I think you're exposed: the site's numbers (">10.000" active users, "90%" average
engagement) are undefined, and sponsors who are asked to justify spend will eventually ask
how engagement is measured. Defining that metric is a small engineering task and a large
sales asset. Where I think there's room: matchmaking and icebreakers are the natural place
for a language model, and also the natural place to get burned, because a suggestion that is
wrong about a person is worse than no suggestion at all. That gap between "LLM feature" and
"LLM feature we can stand behind in front of a sponsor" is the thing I've spent the last
few years on. I may be wrong about both of these; that's what the call is for.

For context: I was research lead at Spring Research (2020–2022), did an LLM research
contract for Move38 this year, and was retained by LevEhad in 2024 through a cloud migration.
On my own I run two Elixir/Phoenix services, an LLM inference harness with multi-provider
routing and bounded concurrency, and a credential gateway in front of the Gemini API; they're
in private repositories and I'm happy to walk you through the code. Public work is at
github.com/marchdown, including co-loop, a fork-based protocol for parallel agentic work.

Would you have thirty minutes in the next two weeks? I'm in Jerusalem, so Stockholm
afternoons work well. Depending on what you need, I can come on part-time as the September ad
describes, do a scoped two-week assessment that produces the two memos above, or work on a
monthly retainer. If you're in Israel or I'm in Stockholm, coffee instead.

Pavel Vasilyev
uprootiny@gmail.com · hyperstitious.org
